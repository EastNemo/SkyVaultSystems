About SkyVault

A storage system designed around control, restraint, and clear boundaries.

Why SkyVault Exists

SkyVault was created in response to a simple observation: most modern storage services require users to trust infrastructure they cannot verify and policies they cannot meaningfully audit.

We believe that secure systems should reduce trust requirements rather than demand them. Encryption should happen before data leaves the user’s device, and key ownership should remain with the user at all times.

Design Principles

Minimize Trust

Systems should remain safe even when infrastructure is inspected, logged, or compromised.

Explicit Boundaries

Clear statements about what the service can and cannot do are more valuable than broad promises.

User Control

Users retain control over encryption keys, data lifecycle, and storage location decisions.

Quiet by Default

SkyVault avoids unnecessary data collection, telemetry, and background processes.

What SkyVault Is Not

  • A general-purpose file sharing platform
  • A social or collaborative storage service
  • A system that can recover lost encryption keys
  • A service that monetizes user data or behavior

Looking Forward

SkyVault is being developed incrementally, with security and correctness taking precedence over rapid feature expansion. New capabilities are introduced only when they can be implemented without weakening the underlying trust model.

As the platform evolves, design decisions, audits, and operational changes will be documented publicly.
